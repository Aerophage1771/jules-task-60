## What changed from the source post, and why

The post keeps the source wording wherever it already worked: the opening, all six family sections, the Change the Argument list, the review question and the consultation close are close to verbatim. The changes:

1. **A map before the territory.** “The Six Families” opens with a three-column table (family, question types, the question to ask first), taken from the complete-map graphic. A reader sees the whole structure before reading any family. On a phone the live reader stacks it into one labelled card per family.
2. **Why the order matters.** The connected-process text from the alternate presentation follows the table: parts lead to description, description to the flaw, the flaw to changing the support, and the fifth family changes your role. The sixth family is explained as separate, not as a later step.
3. **A real demonstration for Change the Argument.** The one-line city example is replaced by the library and Model L argument from “One argument, six tasks”: the argument in a box, the gap stated as a number (M below $4,000), and a table showing what each of the six tasks does with that one gap. This is the clearest proof in the source that helpful, required and sufficient are different, and the $4,000 threshold shows that one statement can be both necessary and sufficient.
4. **The full reference.** “All 20 Types at a Glance” carries the source reference table (type, task, wrong-answer pattern), grouped by family, followed by the source rule that “too strong,” “out of scope” and “new information” are not automatic reasons to eliminate. The method column is left out because the family sections already teach each method and a four-column table scrolls sideways inside the 64-character article column on every screen.
5. **A routine you can run.** The practice section turns the source’s “identify, choose the tool, test” paragraph into a three-step numbered list, and adds the source’s own review prompt for answers that stop at “out of scope” or “too strong.”
6. **Labels that read as sentences.** “What not to do: Choose a statement…” became “The mistake to avoid: choosing a statement…”, so the label no longer reads as an instruction to do the wrong thing.
7. **Small precision fixes.** “Ultimately defending” became a test you can run (follow the support until it reaches a claim that supports nothing further). “Might leave the conclusion unproven” became “might not be enough to prove the conclusion.” The meaning is unchanged; the new wording also avoids words a hype filter could misread.
8. **Links.** “The Weaken examples” now links to the seven Weaken formats post. The consultation link uses `/contact`, the convention in the current posts (the reader maps it to the consultation page). The closing line about “the next post” is gone, because no later post is registered in the repository; its two useful sentences stay.
9. **Frequency, used once and bounded.** One paragraph in the practice section gives the family shares and the Flaw figure from the older unpublished post (5,002 released LR questions, PrepTest 1 through 159; 37, 19, 15, 14, 8 and 7 percent; Flaw 14.9 percent). No other numbers are used. It says frequency decides review time, never method, and why the smallest family still matters. The fixed guide says no percentages are needed to use the map, so if you prefer that framing, delete the two paragraphs that start “Frequency can help” and “A small share”; nothing else depends on them.
10. **No invented claims.** The older post’s “the trap that catches the most students” wording is not used anywhere. The library example is labelled as an original teaching example.

## Preview notes

The full-article export is a review image, not something to post. The export kit cannot evaluate CSS counters, so the preview page adds a rule (outside the post content) that prints 01, 02, 03 on the numbered list in the export, as the live site does. The kit’s renderer also measures a few lines of text slightly differently from the page, so the exported article shows one or two spots with an extra line of space and the “Back to The Fix” button wraps; the live page and the preview in the browser do not.
