### Slide 02: The problem

- Eyebrow: THE PROBLEM
- Headline: A true answer can still be wrong.
- Rows:
  - A statement that strengthens an argument might not be necessary to it.
  - A necessary assumption might leave the conclusion unproven.
  - An accurate description of a sentence might name its topic without identifying its role.
- Closing line: These are differences in the task.

### Slide 03: Family 01

- Eyebrow: FAMILY 01 · IDENTIFY THE PARTS
- Headline: What is each statement doing?
- Rows:
  - Role in Argument: Identify the job of the quoted statement.
  - Main Conclusion: Identify the claim the author is ultimately defending.
- Check: Don’t choose a statement because it is important or true.

### Slide 04: Family 02

- Eyebrow: FAMILY 02 · DESCRIBE THE ARGUMENT
- Headline: How does the reasoning work?
- Rows:
  - Method of Reasoning: Describe how the argument proceeds.
  - Parallel Reasoning: Match the reasoning structure.
- Check: Don’t match topics or familiar words.

### Slide 05: Family 03

- Eyebrow: FAMILY 03 · CRITIQUE THE ARGUMENT
- Headline: Where does the reasoning fail?
- Rows:
  - Flaw: Identify the error in the reasoning.
  - Parallel Flaw: Match the relevant flawed reasoning.
- Check: Don’t pick a familiar flaw because it is a real flaw.

### Slide 06: Family 04

- Eyebrow: FAMILY 04 · CHANGE THE ARGUMENT
- Headline: What happens to the support?
- Rows:
  - Evaluate: Find information useful in assessing the argument.
  - Weaken: Reduce the support for the conclusion.
  - Strengthen: Improve the support for the conclusion.
  - Sufficient Assumption: Make the conclusion follow when the answer is added.
  - Necessary Assumption: Identify what the argument requires.
  - Principle Strengthen: Supply a general rule that helps justify the conclusion.
- Check: Helpful ≠ required ≠ sufficient.

### Slide 07: Family 05

- Eyebrow: FAMILY 05 · FORM YOUR OWN ARGUMENT
- Headline: What do the facts establish?
- Rows:
  - Fill in the Blank: Complete the passage in the role the blank requires.
  - Most Strongly Supported: Identify the claim with the strongest support from the stimulus.
  - Must Be True: Identify a claim guaranteed by the statements.
  - Must Be False: Identify a claim incompatible with the statements.
- Check: Don’t treat possible, supported, guaranteed, and contradicted as one judgment.

### Slide 08: Family 06

- Eyebrow: FAMILY 06 · ARGUMENT-ADJACENT
- Headline: Check both sides.
- Rows:
  - Agree / Disagree: Identify a shared or opposed position.
  - Principle: Apply: Identify the case or judgment licensed by a rule.
  - Principle: Generalize: Identify the principle the case illustrates.
  - Paradox: Resolve, Reconcile, Explain: Explain how the surprising facts can coexist.
- Check: Don’t ignore one side of the relationship.

### Slide 09: Practice

- Eyebrow: PRACTICE
- Headline: Name the task. Then test the choice.
- Steps:
  1. State the task in one sentence before reading the choices.
  2. After a miss, ask: “What standard did my chosen answer fail?”
- Contrast: “Wrong answer” is not much of a diagnosis. “I picked something that strengthened the argument when I needed something the argument required” tells you what to change.

## Caption

A statement can strengthen an argument without being necessary to it. It can be necessary without being enough to prove the conclusion.

If those standards blur together, the same answer can seem right for three different questions.

I organized the 20 Logical Reasoning question types into six families. The first five follow the work you do on an argument: identify its parts, describe it, critique it, change it, and draw a conclusion yourself. The sixth covers two speakers, rules and cases, and apparent conflicts.

The Weaken examples gave you several ways to attack reasoning. This map shows where that skill fits and what changes when the task changes. Swipe through all six families to see what each type asks you to do.

For your next missed question, state the task in one sentence. Then explain which part of that task your selected answer failed.
