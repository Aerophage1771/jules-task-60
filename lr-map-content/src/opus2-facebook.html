LR Question Type Map · Family 4 
Before you decide whether an LSAT answer is good, decide what it needs to do.

Helpful, necessary, and sufficient are different standards. So are possible, supported, guaranteed, and contradicted. If those standards blur together, the same answer can seem right for three different questions.

The image holds one argument fixed: Model L lights will cut a library’s annual electricity bill by exactly $4,000, and nothing else changes except possible added maintenance. The conclusion, that total annual operating costs will fall, holds only if that added maintenance stays under $4,000.

Sufficient Assumption needs a statement that makes the conclusion follow, so add the answer to the premises and check. A $1,000 cap on added maintenance passes: it guarantees at least $3,000 in savings. The argument doesn’t need a cap that tight, though. A $3,000 increase would still save money.

Necessary Assumption needs something the argument requires, so negate the answer and check. “Less than $4,000” passes: $4,000 or more means no reduction. In this example it is sufficient too. A statement can meet more than one standard, and the question stem tells you which one you need. On real questions, the negation doesn’t have to make the conclusion false. Ask whether the reasoning still has the support it requires.

Helpful is a third standard. If three comparable libraries with the same fixtures saw maintenance rise less than $1,000, that strengthens the argument without guaranteeing this library’s cost, and the argument doesn’t need it.

Required, not sufficient.

Negate it first: added maintenance is $5,000 or more. Total costs then rise by at least $1,000, so the argument can’t work without the statement. Now add it to the premises instead. An increase of $4,500 is still less than $5,000, and it raises total costs by $500, so the statement doesn’t make the conclusion follow. On the chart, its bar covers the whole zone where costs fall, then runs past it.

That completes the set. The $1,000 cap is sufficient but not required. “Less than $4,000” is both. “Less than $5,000” is required but not sufficient. Same argument, three statements, two tests: the question stem tells you which test the right answer has to pass.
