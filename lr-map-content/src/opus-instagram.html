Logical Reasoning · The LR Question Type Map

True, helpful, and still wrong.

A statement can strengthen an argument without being necessary to it. It can be necessary without being enough to prove the conclusion.

Helpful

Improves the support.

Required

The argument needs it.

Sufficient

Proves the conclusion.

The question type tells you which standard an answer has to meet. Swipe for all 20 types, a check for each family, and one argument tested six ways.

Family 1 · Identify the Parts

Find the claim the author is ultimately defending.

Role in Argument·Main Conclusion

PremiseGives support.
Intermediate conclusionReceives support, then supports another claim.
Main conclusionWhere the support ends.
The check

Take two candidate conclusions and ask which one the author uses as a reason for the other. On Role in Argument, find the main conclusion first, then locate the quoted statement relative to it.

Watch for

A sentence does not become the main conclusion because it comes last or follows “therefore.” On Role questions, an answer can name the right role and still misstate what that statement supports.

03 / 13
Family 2 · Describe the Argument

Remove the topic. Describe the move.

Method of Reasoning·Parallel Reasoning

State the move in plain language. The author might eliminate alternatives, compare two cases, apply a rule, or show that a proposed explanation conflicts with a fact.

The check: read an answer as a set of claims

“The author cites an expert and questions that expert’s motives.”

Cites an expert
Questions that expert’s motives

If the author never questions motives, the answer fails. It started accurately and finished with a move the author never makes.

Watch for

Matching the topic or familiar words. Two arguments about doctors can reason differently. For Parallel Reasoning, compare the conditions, the direction of support, and the force of the conclusion.

04 / 13
Family 3 · Critique the Argument

Name the gap before you read the flaw answers.

Flaw·Parallel Flaw

Ask what the author needed to establish but left open. What could be true about the situation that would expose the weakness in the inference?

The check: translate the answer back
The answer says

“The argument confuses a requirement with a guarantee.”

You point to

1. The requirement in the stimulus.

2. The claim that treats it as a guarantee.

If you cannot locate that error in the reasoning, knowing the name of the fallacy is not enough.

Watch for

A real flaw that this argument does not commit. In Parallel Flaw, the correct answer must fail in the corresponding way. Another bad argument is not necessarily a parallel bad argument.

Using “this helps” as the standard for every question in the family. These six often begin from the same gap. The next four slides hold one argument fixed and change the task.

Family 4 · Worked example

Hold the argument still. Change the task.

The argument (invented for teaching)

Switching a library’s conventional lights to Model L fixtures will cut its annual electricity bill by exactly $4,000. All other annual operating costs stay unchanged, except that Model L may add maintenance costs.

So the switch will reduce total annual operating costs.

The gap: how much maintenance rises
$0 added$4,000$6,000
Under $4,000: total costs fall. The conclusion holds.
$4,000 or more: no reduction.
Task 1 · Evaluate

Ask how much Model L will add to annual maintenance. An answer of $1,000 supports the conclusion. An answer of $5,000 defeats it. Contrasting responses change the assessment.

07 / 13
Family 4 · Worked example

A weakener need not disprove. A strengthener need not prove.

$4,000 saved · net change = added maintenance minus $4,000

Task 2 · Weaken

“Model L will add $5,000 a year in maintenance.”

That more than cancels the $4,000 saving. This weakener happens to disprove the conclusion in this specified case. Weakening does not always require disproof.

Task 3 · Strengthen

“Three libraries with comparable operating hours and the same fixtures incurred maintenance increases under $1,000.”

That supports a low estimate here. It does not guarantee the estimate, because this library’s conditions could differ.

The check

Accept the answer as true. Then say what it does to the inference, not just to the topic.

08 / 13
Family 4 · Worked example

Required is not the same as enough.

Added annual maintenance
Required: under $4,000
Enough: $1,000 cap
$0$1,000$3,000$4,000$6,000
Task 4 · Necessary Assumption

“Model L will add less than $4,000 to annual maintenance costs.”

Negate it: $4,000 or more. Then total costs do not fall, so the argument requires this.

Task 5 · Sufficient Assumption

“Model L will add no more than $1,000 to annual maintenance costs.”

Add it to the premises: at least $3,000 is saved, so the conclusion follows. It is not necessary. A $3,000 increase (the dashed line) would still allow savings.

09 / 13
Family 4 · Worked example

Connect the facts, the rule, and the conclusion.

Task 6 · Principle Strengthen

“Libraries that replace conventional lights with Model L add no more than $1,000 in annual maintenance.”

The factsThis library is switching from conventional lights to Model L.
The rule appliesSo added maintenance is capped at $1,000.
The conclusionTotal annual costs fall by at least $3,000.
The check

A rule about a different fixture model would not apply merely because its result looks useful. Increased support is enough unless the question asks you to make the conclusion follow.

Across all six tasks

A statement may meet more than one standard. Here, “less than $4,000” is both necessary and sufficient. The stem determines which property you need to establish.

10 / 13
Family 5 · Form Your Own Argument

Supported is not guaranteed. Unknown is not false.

PossibleNot a standard

The facts leave it open. A claim can be plausible without being supported here.

SupportedMost Strongly Supported

You can point to its support in the information provided.

GuaranteedMust Be True

Try to make it false while the stimulus stays true. If you can, it is not guaranteed.

ContradictedMust Be False

It cannot coexist with the facts. Something never discussed may simply be unknown.

Fill in the Blank

Name the blank’s job first. A conclusion needs support from what came before. A missing premise needs to do the work the surrounding argument requires.

11 / 13
Family 6 · Argument-Adjacent

Check both sides of the relationship.

Speaker ASpeaker B
Agree / Disagree

Establish each speaker’s position on the exact statement in the answer. Silence is not disagreement.

RuleCase
Principle: Apply

Check the case against each condition in the rule. Meeting most of a rule’s requirements is not meeting all of them.

CaseRule
Principle: Generalize

Start with the case and find the rule it illustrates. A principle about the same subject can still misdescribe the case.

FactFact
Paradox

State what is surprising: we would expect this, but that happened. A correct explanation lets both facts remain true and makes their coexistence less surprising.

Use the map in review

“Wrong answer” is not a diagnosis.

Next time you review a miss, write three lines:

1 · Question type
2 · The task, in one sentence
3 · The standard my answer failed
A diagnosis you can use

“I picked something that strengthened the argument when I needed something the argument required.”

That tells you what to change.

A statement can strengthen an argument without being necessary to it. It can be necessary without being enough to prove the conclusion.

If those standards blur together, the same answer can seem right for three different questions.

I organized the 20 Logical Reasoning question types into six families. The first five follow the work you do on an argument: identify its parts, describe it, critique it, change it, and draw a conclusion yourself. The sixth covers two speakers, rules and cases, and apparent conflicts.

Slides 7 to 10 hold one library’s cost argument fixed and change the task. The most useful check is on slide 9: a $1,000 cap on added maintenance guarantees savings, but savings do not require that tight a cap.

For your next missed question, state the task in one sentence. Then explain which part of that task your selected answer failed.
