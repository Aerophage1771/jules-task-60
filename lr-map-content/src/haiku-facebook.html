LSAT LOGICAL REASONING

Before you judge an answer, decide what it needs to do.

Change the Argument

Helpful

Strengthen: improves the support for the conclusion.

Required

Necessary Assumption: something the argument requires.

Sufficient

Sufficient Assumption: makes the conclusion follow.

Form Your Own Argument

Possible

The stimulus does not rule it out. That alone proves nothing.

Supported

Most Strongly Supported: the best support in the stimulus.

Guaranteed

Must Be True: cannot be false if the stimulus is true.

Contradicted

Must Be False: cannot coexist with the stimulus.

Before you decide whether an LSAT answer is good, decide what it needs to do.

Helpful, necessary, and sufficient are different standards. So are possible, supported, guaranteed, and contradicted.

A statement can strengthen an argument without being necessary to it. It can be necessary without being enough to prove the conclusion. If those standards blur together, the same answer can seem right for three different questions.
